## 1. Purpose, Scope, and Ethical Commitment

1.1 The organization expects all representatives to conduct business
honestly, fairly, respectfully, lawfully, and in the best interests of
the organization and its stakeholders.

1.2 This policy establishes minimum standards for ethical behavior,
conflicts of interest, outside activities, gifts and hospitality,
anti-bribery, fair competition, insider information, reporting,
investigations, and non-retaliation.

1.3 It applies in the workplace, during business travel, at customer and
supplier sites, online, at company events, and whenever a person
represents or acts for the organization.

1.4 The organization prohibits retaliation against anyone who raises a
concern in good faith or participates in an investigation.

## 2. Definitions

-   **Conflict of interest:** A personal, financial, family, or outside
    interest that could improperly influence, or appear to influence,
    professional judgment.
-   **Bribe / improper advantage:** Anything of value offered, promised,
    given, requested, or received to improperly influence a decision or
    secure an advantage.
-   **Facilitation payment:** A payment intended to expedite a routine
    government action; prohibited by this policy even where local
    practice tolerates it, unless Legal authorizes an action necessary
    to address an imminent threat to life or safety and documents it.
-   **Gift:** A tangible item, benefit, discount, service, or thing of
    value provided without full payment.
-   **Hospitality:** Meals, travel, accommodation, entertainment, event
    tickets, or similar benefits.
-   **Government official:** A person covered by applicable anti-bribery
    laws, including relevant public employees, officials, candidates,
    employees of state-owned entities, and public international
    organization personnel.
-   **MNPI:** Material non-public information that a reasonable investor
    would consider important or that could affect the price or value of
    securities.
-   **Good-faith report:** A concern raised honestly, based on
    information the reporter reasonably believes may indicate
    misconduct, even if the concern is not ultimately substantiated.

## 3. Roles and Responsibilities

### 3.1 Everyone

All covered persons must read and follow this policy, complete required
training, disclose conflicts, preserve accurate records, cooperate with
investigations, and report suspected violations.

### 3.2 Managers

Managers must model ethical behavior, escalate concerns, protect
reporters from retaliation, and never discourage or investigate a
serious allegation informally without Compliance direction.

### 3.3 Compliance and Legal

Compliance maintains guidance, registers, risk assessments, and
investigation procedures. Legal advises on legal obligations, privilege,
regulatory reporting, and conflicts.

### 3.4 HR

HR supports respectful-workplace investigations, employee protections,
discipline, and training while maintaining confidentiality.

### 3.5 Board / Audit Committee

The appropriate independent governance body oversees allegations
involving senior executives, directors, material financial misconduct,
or conflicts affecting executive leadership.

## 4. Respectful Workplace, Equal Opportunity, and Harassment Prevention

4.1 Discrimination, harassment, bullying, intimidation, and
microaggressions are prohibited on the basis of race, gender, sexual
orientation, disability, religion, age, military status, or any other
status protected by applicable law.

4.2 Decisions about recruitment, pay, promotion, training, discipline,
work allocation, and termination must be based on legitimate job-related
criteria and applied consistently.

4.3 Unwelcome conduct may be verbal, physical, written, visual, digital,
or conduct through messaging and social platforms. Retaliation, threats,
repeated humiliation, and hostile conduct are prohibited.

4.4 Employees may report concerns to HR, Compliance, a manager, or the
confidential reporting channels in Section 12. A person need not report
to the alleged offender or their direct supervisor.

4.5 Reports must be assessed promptly and fairly. The organization will
take proportionate corrective action where misconduct is substantiated.

## 5. Conflicts of Interest and Outside Activities

### 5.1 Disclosure Duty

Employees must avoid actual, potential, and perceived conflicts and
disclose them promptly before participating in a related decision or
transaction.

### 5.2 Examples

Conflicts may arise from: - A personal or family relationship with a
supplier, customer, competitor, candidate, or subordinate. - Financial
interests in a vendor, supplier, or competitor. - Selecting a business
in which the employee or family member has an interest. - Accepting
gifts, travel, hospitality, or favors that may influence judgment. -
Using company opportunities, property, data, or confidential information
for personal gain. - Supervising or influencing the employment decisions
of a close relative or partner.

### 5.3 Outside Employment and Secondary Activities

Employees may not engage in secondary employment, advisory roles, or
paid consulting for a direct competitor, customer, or vendor of the
company.

Side projects, moonlighting, and open-source software development: -
Must not use company time, hardware, confidential information, funds, or
intellectual property. - Must not interfere with job duties or create a
conflict. - Require formal written clearance from Legal through the
Outside Activity Disclosure Form.

### 5.4 Personal Investments

Holding an equity interest of **more than 1%** in a non-public vendor,
supplier, or commercial competitor requires immediate disclosure to the
Chief Compliance Officer. Other investments may also require disclosure
where they create an actual or perceived conflict.

### 5.5 Review and Management

Compliance/Legal will assess the facts and may approve, restrict,
require recusal, change reporting lines, impose safeguards, or prohibit
the activity. Employees must follow the written decision and update
disclosures when circumstances change.

## 6. Gifts, Hospitality, and Anti-Bribery

### 6.1 Zero-Tolerance Rule

Direct or indirect offering, giving, soliciting, or receiving of bribes,
kickbacks, payoffs, or improper facilitation payments involving
government officials, commercial clients, suppliers, or partners is
prohibited.

### 6.2 Gift and Hospitality Thresholds

### 6.3 Additional Controls

-   Never offer or accept a benefit intended to influence a procurement,
    hiring, regulatory, inspection, licensing, or commercial decision.
-   Gifts to or from government officials require prior Legal/Compliance
    review regardless of value.
-   Split gifts, indirect gifts, gifts to family members, and benefits
    routed through third parties are treated as gifts to the intended
    recipient.
-   Hospitality must have a legitimate business purpose, reasonable
    attendees, and appropriate documentation.
-   Company funds may not be used for political contributions or
    charitable donations to obtain an improper business advantage.

### 6.4 Records

All reportable gifts and hospitality must be recorded accurately,
including giver/recipient, organization, value, date, business purpose,
approver, and disposition.

### 6.5 Third Parties

Agents, distributors, consultants, and intermediaries must undergo
risk-based due diligence and receive anti-bribery terms and training
appropriate to their role. Payments must match documented services and
be made to verified contractual parties.

## 7. Fair Competition and Antitrust

7.1 Employees must compete fairly and must not agree or coordinate with
competitors to: - Fix or coordinate prices, discounts, margins, or
commercial terms. - Rig bids or allocate tenders. - Divide customers,
territories, products, or markets. - Coordinate wages, hiring, or
employee non-solicitation in ways prohibited by law. - Exchange
competitively sensitive information without lawful basis and Legal
approval.

7.2 Trade association meetings, industry events, and informal competitor
discussions require care. If a prohibited topic arises, stop the
discussion, leave where appropriate, and report it to Legal.

7.3 Employees must not obtain competitor information through deception,
unauthorized access, theft, or breach of confidentiality.

## 8. Insider Trading and Confidential Information

8.1 Employees who possess MNPI about the organization, a customer,
supplier, partner, or other issuer must not trade related securities,
recommend trades, or disclose the information to another person except
through an authorized business process.

8.2 MNPI may include non-public financial results, major contracts,
acquisitions, material product/security events, executive changes, or
other information likely to influence an investor's decision.

8.3 Employees must follow any trading windows, restricted lists,
pre-clearance procedures, and confidentiality restrictions issued by
Legal.

8.4 A person who is uncertain whether information is material or public
must contact Legal before trading or sharing it.

## 9. Accurate Books, Records, and Company Property

9.1 All business transactions must be recorded accurately, completely,
and in the correct period. False invoices, hidden accounts, misleading
descriptions, fabricated expenses, and off-book payments are prohibited.

9.2 Employees must protect company money, assets, systems, intellectual
property, and confidential information.

9.3 Records must not be altered, destroyed, or concealed to avoid audit,
investigation, or legal obligations.

9.4 Expenses, gifts, charitable contributions, commissions, and
third-party payments must be supported by legitimate business purpose
and appropriate approval.

## 10. Confidentiality, Privacy, and Information Security

10.1 Employees must access information only for authorized duties and
follow the Information Security, Customer Data Privacy, and BYOD
policies.

10.2 Confidential information must not be disclosed to unauthorized
persons, used for personal benefit, or entered into unapproved public AI
tools.

10.3 Suspected data loss, cybersecurity incidents, privacy breaches, or
unauthorized access must be reported immediately through the designated
Security channel.

## 11. Reporting, Investigation, and Cooperation

11.1 Reports may concern suspected fraud, bribery, discrimination,
harassment, conflicts, retaliation, financial irregularity, security
vulnerabilities, or legal violations.

11.2 Reports may be made through the channels in Section 12. Anonymous
reporting is permitted through the online portal where available and
legally permitted.

11.3 Compliance will assess urgency, conflicts, evidence preservation,
safety, confidentiality, and the need for independent investigation.

11.4 Investigations should be timely, impartial, documented, and
proportionate. The subject of an allegation must be treated fairly, and
findings must be based on evidence.

11.5 Employees must preserve relevant documents and cooperate honestly.
Destroying evidence, coaching witnesses, or obstructing an investigation
is prohibited.

11.6 Information is shared only with those who need it to investigate,
respond, or meet legal obligations. Absolute confidentiality cannot be
guaranteed where disclosure is required by law or a fair investigation.

## 12. Whistleblower Protection and Reporting Channels

### 12.1 Non-Retaliation

A person who reports in good faith or participates in an investigation
must not suffer threats, harassment, demotion, dismissal, reduced hours,
exclusion, or other adverse action because of the report.

12.2 Retaliation is a separate policy violation and must be reported
immediately.

### 12.3 Source Reporting Channels

-   **Confidential Ethics Hotline:** 24/7 toll-free hotline:
    `1-800-ETHICS-LINE`
-   **Online Anonymous Portal:** `https://ethics.company.com/report`
-   **Email:** `compliance-officer@company.com`

**Contact verification:** The source channels are retained exactly as
supplied but appear to be placeholder-style contact details.
Compliance/IT must validate that the hotline, portal, and mailbox are
active, monitored, secure, and accessible before publication.

### 12.4 Emergency or Immediate Threat

If there is an immediate threat to life, safety, or physical security,
contact local emergency services first and then notify the appropriate
company channel.

### 12.5 External Reporting Rights

Nothing in this policy prevents a person from reporting to a regulator,
law-enforcement authority, or other body where protected by applicable
law.

## 13. Disciplinary Action and Remediation

13.1 Substantiated violations may result in coaching, training, warning,
removal of approval authority, access restrictions, suspension,
termination, contract termination, recovery of losses, or referral to
authorities, depending on severity and law.

13.2 Discipline must be proportionate, consistent, documented, and
subject to fair process.

13.3 The organization should address root causes through control
improvements, training, vendor remediation, process redesign, and
monitoring.

## 14. Training, Monitoring, and Review

14.1 All covered persons must complete conduct and anti-bribery training
at onboarding and periodically thereafter. Higher-risk roles require
enhanced training.

14.2 Compliance should monitor gift-register completeness, conflict
disclosures, third-party due diligence, hotline trends, training
completion, and investigation closure.

14.3 This policy is reviewed annually and when laws, business
operations, or risk exposure materially change.

## 15. Policy RAG and Interpretation Rules

-   Quote the exact gift threshold and explain that cash equivalents are
    prohibited at every value.
-   Do not treat a gift below \$50 as automatically acceptable if it
    could influence a decision or involve a government official.
-   Distinguish actual, potential, and perceived conflicts.
-   Do not promise anonymity or absolute confidentiality; describe the
    reporting channel accurately.
-   Do not invent investigation outcomes or disciplinary penalties for a
    specific person.
-   If a contact channel is requested, state that the supplied channel
    must be verified before operational use.
-   If a topic is not covered, say **"This policy does not specify that
    scenario; contact Compliance or Legal before acting."**
