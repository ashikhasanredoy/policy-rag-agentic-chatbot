## 1. Purpose, Scope, and Policy Statement

### 1.1 Purpose

This policy establishes mandatory controls for collecting, using,
accessing, sharing, storing, transferring, retaining, and securely
disposing of customer personal data. It is intended to protect
individuals, maintain customer trust, reduce privacy risk, and
demonstrate accountable data governance.

### 1.2 Scope

This policy applies to: - Customer, prospective-customer, account, user,
billing, support, and transaction information. - Personal data processed
through websites, mobile applications, APIs, customer-support platforms,
analytics tools, cloud services, and offline business processes. -
Production, test, development, backup, archive, and disaster-recovery
environments. - All personnel and third parties who can access or
process personal data for the organization.

It applies regardless of whether information is stored electronically,
on paper, in recordings, or in another format.

### 1.3 Policy Statement

Personal data may be processed only for a defined business purpose,
under an identified lawful basis where required, with appropriate
transparency, access restrictions, security safeguards, retention
controls, and documented accountability. Convenience, curiosity, or an
employee's access capability is never a valid reason to access customer
data.

### 1.4 Privacy Principles

1.  **Lawfulness, fairness, and transparency:** Explain processing
    clearly and use an appropriate legal basis.
2.  **Purpose limitation:** Use data only for specified, legitimate
    purposes. A materially different secondary purpose requires a
    documented compatibility assessment and, where required, a new
    notice or consent.
3.  **Data minimization:** Collect and expose only the minimum
    information needed.
4.  **Accuracy:** Keep personal data accurate and provide a correction
    route.
5.  **Storage limitation:** Retain personal data only for an approved
    period or documented legal/business need.
6.  **Integrity and confidentiality:** Protect data against unauthorized
    access, alteration, loss, and disclosure.
7.  **Accountability:** Maintain evidence that privacy requirements are
    implemented and monitored.

## 2. Definitions

-   **Personal data / PII:** Information relating to an identified or
    identifiable individual. PII is used in this policy as a practical
    label and does not replace jurisdiction-specific legal definitions.
-   **Sensitive or special-category data:** Data receiving heightened
    legal protection, such as health, biometric, racial or ethnic,
    religious, political, or other specially protected information under
    applicable law.
-   **Processing:** Any operation performed on personal data, including
    collection, viewing, analysis, storage, disclosure, transfer, or
    deletion.
-   **Data subject:** The individual to whom personal data relates.
-   **Controller / business:** The entity that determines the purposes
    and means of processing.
-   **Processor / service provider:** A party processing personal data
    on behalf of a controller or business.
-   **Sub-processor:** A processor engaged by another processor to
    perform processing activities.
-   **DSAR / privacy request:** A request by an individual to exercise
    an applicable privacy right.
-   **De-identification / anonymization:** Techniques that reduce or
    remove the ability to identify an individual. Data is anonymous only
    when identification is not reasonably possible under the applicable
    legal test.
-   **Pseudonymization:** Replacing direct identifiers with codes while
    retaining a separately protected means of re-identification.
-   **Data breach:** A security incident leading to accidental or
    unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of,
    or access to personal data.

## 3. Governance and Responsibilities

### 3.1 Privacy / Data Protection Lead

The Privacy Lead shall: - Maintain this policy, privacy notices,
processing records, and request procedures. - Advise business owners on
lawful bases, transparency, minimization, and retention. - Coordinate
privacy impact assessments and regulatory inquiries. - Track DSAR
deadlines, complaints, and remediation actions. - Escalate material
privacy risks to Legal and executive leadership.

Where a statutory Data Protection Officer (DPO) is required or formally
appointed, that role's statutory independence and responsibilities must
be respected.

### 3.2 Business and Product Owners

Business owners shall document why personal data is needed, the data
elements collected, users with access, recipients, retention periods,
and deletion triggers. New or materially changed processing must be
reviewed before launch.

### 3.3 Engineering and IT

Engineering and IT shall implement approved access controls, encryption,
logging, backup, deletion, and secure development controls. They shall
not create production copies for testing without approval and
safeguards.

### 3.4 Employees and Contractors

Personnel shall access personal data only for assigned work, follow
approved procedures, protect credentials, complete required training,
and report suspected incidents immediately.

### 3.5 Procurement and Vendor Owners

Vendor owners shall complete privacy and security due diligence before a
third party receives personal data and shall ensure an appropriate Data
Processing Agreement (DPA) is executed.

## 4. Data Inventory, Collection, and Lawful Processing

### 4.1 Processing Inventory

Each business owner must maintain or contribute to a record of
processing that identifies: - Processing purpose and business owner. -
Categories of individuals and personal data. - Source of the data and
collection channel. - Legal basis or applicable business purpose. -
Internal users, recipients, processors, and transfer locations. -
Retention rule and deletion method. - Security classification and key
safeguards.

### 4.2 Collection Requirements

Before collecting data, the owner must confirm that: 1. The data is
necessary for a specific purpose. 2. The collection point provides an
appropriate privacy notice. 3. Required consent or other lawful basis is
established and recorded. 4. Optional fields are clearly distinguished
from mandatory fields. 5. Sensitive data is not collected unless
specifically approved and legally permitted.

Do not collect full payment-card details, government identifiers,
precise location, health information, or other high-risk data merely
because a system can accept it.

### 4.3 Lawful Basis and Consent

For GDPR/UK GDPR processing, the responsible owner and Privacy/Legal
must identify and document an applicable lawful basis, such as contract,
legal obligation, legitimate interests, consent, vital interests, or
public task where relevant. Consent must be specific, informed, freely
given, unambiguous, and withdrawable where consent is the basis.

For California and other US privacy laws, the business must identify
applicable notice, opt-out, sensitive-data, sale/share, and
service-provider requirements. Do not describe all processing as
"consent-based" unless that is legally and factually correct.

### 4.4 Secondary Use

Customer data collected for order fulfillment, account administration,
support, or another stated purpose must not be reused for unrelated
marketing, profiling, sale, sharing, or product development without a
documented legal assessment and any required notice, consent, or opt-out
mechanism.

### 4.5 Privacy by Design

Privacy review must occur during product and process design, not only
after launch. New high-risk processing, systematic monitoring,
large-scale sensitive-data use, or significant automated decision-making
must be referred to Privacy/Legal for a Data Protection Impact
Assessment (DPIA) or equivalent assessment where required.

## 5. Transparency, Notices, Cookies, and Customer Choice

5.1 Privacy notices must explain, in clear language, the organization
responsible for processing, data categories, purposes, legal bases where
applicable, recipients, international transfers, retention criteria,
individual rights, and contact channels.

5.2 Notices must be available at or before the point of collection, or
through another legally permitted method.

5.3 Marketing preferences must be recorded and respected across relevant
systems. Opt-outs and consent withdrawals must be propagated to
applicable marketing and customer-engagement platforms within a
documented operational timeframe.

5.4 Cookies, pixels, SDKs, and similar tracking technologies must be
reviewed for purpose, data collected, vendor access, and consent/opt-out
requirements before deployment. Non-essential tracking must not be
activated where prior consent is legally required.

5.5 Do not use dark patterns, preselected optional consent, or
misleading wording to obtain personal data or weaken privacy choices.

## 6. Data Subject Rights and DSAR Procedure

### 6.1 Rights

Depending on applicable law and circumstances, individuals may have
rights to: - Access personal data and receive required information. -
Correct inaccurate or incomplete data. - Delete or erase personal data,
subject to lawful exceptions. - Restrict or object to certain
processing. - Receive portable data in a structured, commonly used,
machine-readable format where applicable. - Withdraw consent where
consent is the legal basis. - Opt out of sale, sharing, or targeted
advertising where applicable. - Limit certain uses of sensitive personal
information where applicable. - Appeal or complain about a decision
where required by law.

No employee may promise that every record will always be deleted. Legal
retention, legal claims, fraud prevention, security, and other statutory
exceptions may apply.

### 6.2 Intake and Verification

Formal requests should be sent to **privacy@company.com**. The Privacy
Lead must verify the requester's identity using a proportionate method.
Do not request excessive identity documents. Authorized-agent requests
must be validated where applicable.

### 6.3 Internal Service Level and Statutory Deadlines

The source policy establishes an internal target of **thirty (30)
calendar days** from receipt for verified DSARs. The applicable
statutory deadline and permitted extension rules must always be checked
for the request's jurisdiction and right. Where the law requires a
different deadline, that legal deadline controls. For example, GDPR/UK
GDPR generally use a one-month response period, while California law
generally provides a 45-day period subject to applicable extension
rules. The internal 30-day target is an operational target, not a
replacement for legal deadline analysis.

### 6.4 Workflow

1.  Log the request, receipt date, request type, jurisdiction, and due
    date.
2.  Acknowledge receipt where appropriate.
3.  Verify identity and authority.
4.  Search relevant systems, archives, support platforms, and
    processors.
5.  Review results for scope, third-party rights, privilege, security,
    and legal exceptions.
6.  Obtain Legal approval for any refusal, restriction, or extension.
7.  Deliver the response securely in an accessible format.
8.  Record the action, systems searched, decision, delivery date, and
    evidence.
9.  Notify processors or service providers where required to action the
    request.

### 6.5 Refusal or Extension

A request may be refused, limited, or extended only where permitted by
applicable law. The reason, legal basis, approval, and appeal/complaint
route must be documented and communicated within the required period.

## 7. Data Classification, Access, and Security

7.1 Customer personal data must be classified at least as
**Confidential**; sensitive personal data, authentication secrets, and
high-risk identifiers must be treated as **Restricted / Strictly
Confidential**.

7.2 Access must be role-based, need-to-know, approved by the data owner,
protected by MFA where supported, and removed promptly when no longer
required.

7.3 Production access must be logged. Privileged access must be
separately controlled and reviewed. Employees may not browse customer
records outside their assigned duties.

7.4 Personal data must be encrypted in transit and at rest using
approved, current cryptographic controls. The companion Information
Security Policy defines the organization's baseline, including TLS 1.3
and AES-256 where supported and approved.

7.5 Personal data must not be placed in public repositories, personal
email, unapproved AI tools, consumer file-sharing accounts, or unmanaged
removable media.

7.6 Test data must be synthetic or appropriately de-identified wherever
feasible. Production data used in testing requires documented approval,
restricted access, and a defined deletion date.

### 8.2 Retention Administration

The data owner must identify the start event, retention period, system
of record, archive location, and deletion owner. If two schedules apply
to the same record, retain only the elements required for the longer
lawful period and restrict their use.

### 8.3 Legal Holds

Legal may suspend routine deletion where records are relevant to
litigation, investigation, audit, regulatory inquiry, or a legal
preservation duty. The hold must identify scope, owner, reason, and
release authority.

### 8.4 Deletion

Deletion must cover active systems and, where technically and
contractually feasible, replicas, exports, and processor-held copies.
Backup copies may age out through documented backup cycles, provided
they remain protected, are not restored for ordinary use, and are
re-deleted or brought under the applicable retention control after
restoration.

### 8.5 Evidence

System owners must retain deletion logs, job reports, exception
approvals, and vendor confirmations sufficient to demonstrate
completion.

## 9. International Transfers and Third-Party Processing

9.1 Transfers of EEA or UK personal data to a country without an
applicable adequacy decision must use a valid transfer mechanism. The
source policy requires executed EU Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs)
and a Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA) for relevant transfers outside
the EEA. UK transfers may require the UK International Data Transfer
Agreement or UK Addendum, as applicable.

9.2 A TIA must assess destination-country laws and practices, the data,
recipient, access risks, supplementary measures, and whether the
transfer can proceed.

9.3 All cloud sub-processors and third-party vendors processing customer
data must sign an approved DPA before processing begins. The DPA must
address documented instructions, confidentiality, security,
sub-processors, assistance with rights requests and incidents,
deletion/return, audits, and international transfers as applicable.

9.4 Vendor owners must maintain an inventory of processors, locations,
services, data categories, contract status, and review dates. Material
vendor changes require reassessment.

## 10. Privacy Incident and Breach Management

10.1 Personnel must report suspected loss, unauthorized access,
misdirected email, exposed credentials, accidental disclosure,
ransomware, or improper data use immediately through the Security
Incident channel.

10.2 The incident team must: - Contain access or disclosure without
destroying evidence. - Preserve relevant logs and records. - Identify
affected data, individuals, systems, processors, and jurisdictions. -
Assess likelihood and severity of harm. - Involve Privacy, Legal,
Security, and the business owner. - Decide whether regulator, customer,
or individual notification is required. - Document decisions, timelines,
and corrective actions.

10.3 The GDPR's 72-hour supervisory-authority notification requirement
applies where the legal threshold is met; it is not a universal deadline
for every incident or every customer notice. Legal determines
notification obligations and deadlines under each applicable law.

10.4 Do not independently contact customers, regulators, or media about
a suspected breach unless authorized by the incident lead or Legal.

## 11. Training, Monitoring, and Compliance

11.1 Personnel with access to customer data must complete privacy
training at onboarding and at least annually, with role-specific
training for support, engineering, analytics, and privileged users.

11.2 Privacy controls may be tested through access reviews, DSAR
sampling, retention-job validation, vendor reviews, notice audits, and
incident exercises.

11.3 Suggested control metrics: - DSARs completed within applicable
deadlines. - Percentage of processing activities with an owner and
documented purpose. - Overdue retention/deletion tasks. - Percentage of
active processors with current DPAs and risk reviews. - Privacy
incidents by severity and time to containment. - Completion rate for
required privacy training.

11.4 Violations may result in access suspension, retraining,
disciplinary action up to termination, contract remedies, or legal
action, subject to applicable law and due process.

## 12. Exceptions, Records, and Review

12.1 Exceptions require a documented business reason, data owner
approval, Privacy and Security review, compensating controls, expiry
date, and Legal approval where law is affected. No exception may waive a
statutory right.

12.2 Required records include processing inventories, notices, consent
records, DSAR logs, DPIAs, DPAs, transfer assessments, retention
schedules, deletion evidence, incident records, and exception approvals.

12.3 Review this policy at least annually and whenever a material change
occurs in law, business model, technology, data categories, or
third-party processing.
