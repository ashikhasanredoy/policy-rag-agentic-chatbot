## 1. Purpose and Guiding Principles

### 1.1 Purpose

This policy defines how employees plan, approve, book, pay for,
document, submit, and reconcile business travel and business expenses.

### 1.2 Principles

All expenditure must: - Have a legitimate, documented business
purpose. - Be reasonable, necessary, economical, and consistent with
this policy. - Be approved before commitment where required. - Be
supported by accurate records and receipts. - Be charged to the correct
cost center, project, client, and accounting period. - Avoid personal
benefit, duplicate reimbursement, fraud, or conflicts of interest.

Employees must use company funds with the same care they would use for
their own funds. A manager's approval does not make an otherwise
prohibited or unlawful expense reimbursable.

### 1.3 Scope

This policy covers airfare, rail, lodging, meals, ground transportation,
rental cars, mileage, client entertainment, travel-related supplies,
corporate cards, cash advances, and expense reporting. Ordinary
commuting between home and a permanent office is not business travel.

## 2. Definitions

-   **Business travel:** Travel away from the employee's normal work
    location for an approved company purpose.
-   **Pre-approval:** Written authorization obtained before booking or
    incurring the commitment.
-   **Per diem:** A fixed daily meal allowance subject to this policy's
    limits and eligibility conditions.
-   **Itemized receipt:** A receipt showing the items purchased, date,
    vendor, taxes, and total; a card slip showing only a total is not
    sufficient.
-   **Lowest logical airfare:** The lowest practical fare that meets
    business timing, safety, reasonable connection, and schedule
    requirements.
-   **Cost center owner:** Manager accountable for the budget charged.
-   **Corporate card:** Company-issued payment card for authorized
    business expenses only.
-   **Exception:** A documented departure from a standard rule, approved
    by the designated authority before expenditure unless an emergency
    makes advance approval impossible.

## 3. Roles and Responsibilities

### 3.1 Employee / Traveler

The traveler must plan early, obtain required approvals, use approved
booking channels, choose economical options, protect company property,
keep receipts, submit expenses on time, and disclose personal or
mixed-purpose elements.

### 3.2 Direct Manager / Department Head

The manager confirms business necessity, budget availability, traveler
eligibility, dates, cost center, and compliance with approval
thresholds. The approver must not approve their own expenses.

### 3.3 Finance

Finance maintains limits, checks documentation, validates coding and
policy compliance, monitors corporate cards, processes eligible
reimbursements, and escalates suspicious or duplicate claims.

### 3.4 Procurement / Travel Management

The designated travel team maintains preferred suppliers, booking
channels, traveler profiles, and travel-support procedures.

### 3.5 Finance Director / Executive Approver

The designated senior approver authorizes specified cash advances,
premium exceptions, and escalated expenditure in accordance with the
approval matrix.

## 4. Business Need, Budget, and Pre-Approval

4.1 Travel must be necessary to perform a defined business activity that
cannot reasonably be completed through remote communication or a
lower-cost alternative.

4.2 **Written pre-authorization is required** through the Travel
Management Portal for: - Any business trip with projected total cost
**exceeding \$1,000 USD**; or - Any international business travel,
regardless of projected cost.

4.3 The Department Head must approve covered travel at least **fourteen
(14) days before booking**. Approval must identify the business purpose,
traveler, destination, dates, estimated cost, cost center, and any
requested exception.

4.4 The employee must obtain approval before making non-refundable
bookings. Splitting a trip or expense to avoid an approval threshold is
prohibited.

4.5 Travel booked within seven (7) days of departure requires a written
business justification. The justification does not replace any required
approval.

4.6 If an emergency prevents advance approval, the traveler must notify
the manager as soon as practicable and obtain written retrospective
review. Retrospective approval is not guaranteed.

## 5. Air and Rail Transportation

### 5.1 Airfare Class

-   Domestic and short-haul international flights **under five (5)
    hours** must be booked in **Economy / Standard Coach**.
-   Premium Economy is permitted for flights **between five (5) and
    seven (7) hours**, subject to availability and reasonable fare
    comparison.
-   Non-stop international flights **exceeding seven (7) continuous
    hours** are eligible for Business Class only with **Vice President
    (VP) pre-approval**.
-   Economy remains acceptable where it is the most economical
    reasonable option. A higher class is not automatic merely because an
    employee is eligible.

### 5.2 Booking Window and Channel

Flights must be booked at least **fourteen (14) calendar days** before
departure through the designated Corporate Travel Agency (Navan /
Concur) unless an approved exception applies. Bookings made within seven
days require written justification.

### 5.3 Fare Selection

Choose the lowest logical airfare within a **two-hour departure
window**, considering total travel time, necessary connections, safety,
business schedule, baggage requirements, and reasonable cancellation
terms. A cheaper fare is not logical if it creates disproportionate
operational risk or cost.

### 5.4 Loyalty Programs and Upgrades

Employees may retain personal frequent-flyer miles and loyalty points.
Airline selection must not be distorted by personal rewards.
Company-paid upgrades are prohibited unless a written exception is
approved by the VP for a documented business or accessibility reason.

### 5.5 Rail

-   Standard Class is required for rail journeys under **three (3)
    hours**.
-   First Class may be used for journeys exceeding three hours where
    working facilities are required and the additional cost is
    reasonable.
-   Rail must be considered where it is practical, safe, and
    cost-effective.

### 5.6 Changes and Cancellations

Cancel or change unused bookings promptly. Refunds, credits, vouchers,
and unused tickets purchased with company funds belong to the company
and must be recorded in the travel system. Personal extensions and
upgrades must be separately paid by the employee.

## 6. Lodging and Accommodation

6.1 Employees must book one standard single-occupancy room at a
company-preferred partner hotel where practical.

6.2 Maximum nightly room rates, excluding mandatory local taxes: -
**Standard Tier Cities:** up to **\$200 USD per night**. - **Tier-1
High-Cost Metro Cities** --- New York City, San Francisco, London,
Tokyo, Singapore, Zurich, and Paris: up to **\$280 USD per night**. -
**Authorized conference/event hotels:** the negotiated conference rate
may exceed the standard cap by up to **20%**.

6.3 If no safe, suitable hotel is available within the cap, obtain
written pre-approval from the Department Head or Finance before booking.
Provide evidence of reasonable alternatives and pricing.

6.4 Non-reimbursable hotel charges include: - In-room pay-per-view
movies. - Minibar alcohol and snacks. - Spa services. - Laundry, unless
the trip exceeds **five (5) consecutive business days**. - Fitness club
day fees.

6.5 Room upgrades, companion costs, personal entertainment, damage
caused by misconduct, and personal extensions are not reimbursable
unless a documented exception is approved.

6.6 Employees must follow hotel safety instructions, protect room keys
and company devices, and report security or safety incidents promptly.

## 7. Meals and Per Diem

### 7.1 Overnight Business Travel

For approved business travel requiring an overnight stay, the combined
daily meal reimbursement is capped at **\$75 USD per day**: - Breakfast:
up to **\$15 USD**, only if not included in the hotel room rate or
conference schedule. - Lunch: up to **\$25 USD**. - Dinner: up to **\$35
USD**.

The combined daily total must not exceed \$75. Unused allowance in one
meal category cannot be transferred to another category unless Finance
has issued a written local rule.

### 7.2 Alcohol

Alcoholic beverages consumed during individual meals are not
reimbursable under standard per diem rules.

### 7.3 Same-Day Travel

A day trip without an overnight stay qualifies only for lunch
reimbursement up to **\$20 USD**, and only where travel extends beyond
the normal working day by at least three hours.

### 7.4 Included Meals and Special Diets

Do not claim a meal that is included in airfare, lodging, a conference,
or another paid event. Reasonable medically required dietary costs
should be referred to HR/Finance for an accommodation review.

### 7.5 Per Diem Evidence

The expense report must show travel dates, destination, business
purpose, and eligibility. Finance may require additional evidence where
local tax rules require it.

## 8. Ground Transportation and Rental Vehicles

8.1 Preferred ground transportation is public transit, airport shuttle,
or standard ridesharing such as UberX or Lyft Standard where available.

8.2 Premium rideshare categories such as Uber Black or Lyft Lux are not
reimbursable unless required for a documented accessibility, safety, or
business reason and approved in writing.

8.3 Rental vehicles are allowed only when public transit or ridesharing
is demonstrably impractical or more expensive, such as multi-site rural
client visits. The approved category is **Intermediate / Compact
Sedan**. SUVs and luxury vehicles require executive pre-approval.

8.4 For domestic US rentals, employees must decline collision damage
waiver/loss damage waiver (CDW/LDW) where the Corporate Travel Insurance
Policy provides the coverage. For international rentals, accept locally
mandatory CDW insurance. Travelers must confirm coverage with the travel
provider before declining insurance.

8.5 Personal vehicle business mileage is reimbursed at the policy rate
of **\$0.67 USD per mile for 2026**, subject to annual Finance
verification and any higher or mandatory local statutory rate. Commuting
from home to the permanent office is not reimbursable.

8.6 Mileage claims must record date, business purpose, origin,
destination, business miles, and any tolls or parking. Ordinary
commuting miles must be excluded.

8.7 Traffic fines, parking penalties, personal detours, and avoidable
damage caused by negligent use are not reimbursable.

## 9. Client Entertainment and Business Meals

9.1 Client meals and entertainment must have a clear, legitimate
business purpose and must not be used to influence a decision
improperly.

9.2 Client dining is capped at **\$125 USD per attendee**, inclusive of
food, moderate alcoholic beverages, tax, and gratuity. Events exceeding
this amount require Director approval before the event.

9.3 Itemized documentation must include: - Itemized invoice/receipt. -
Business purpose and date. - Names, titles, and organizations of
attendees. - Employee host and cost center. - Any required pre-approval.

9.4 Where multiple company employees attend, the most senior employee
present must pay and submit the expense report.

9.5 Gifts, entertainment, public officials, healthcare professionals,
procurement decision-makers, and other higher-risk recipients must also
comply with the Code of Business Conduct and anti-bribery rules. If the
rules conflict, the stricter rule applies.

## 10. Expense Submission and Reimbursement

10.1 All travel expense reports must be submitted through Concur within
**thirty (30) calendar days** after the trip ends.

10.2 Reports submitted between **31 and 60 days** require Department
Head exception approval and a reason for the delay.

10.3 Claims submitted **after sixty (60) days** are automatically
rejected under the source policy, except where Finance/Legal determines
that applicable law requires different treatment or an approved
exceptional circumstance applies.

10.4 Itemized receipts are mandatory for every individual transaction of
**\$25 USD or more**. A card charge slip showing only a total is
insufficient. Finance may request receipts below \$25 for audit, tax, or
fraud review.

10.5 Each report must include the business purpose, dates, locations,
cost center, attendees where relevant, currency, itemized receipts, and
pre-approval reference.

10.6 Foreign-currency expenses are converted using the official exchange
rate on the transaction date or the actual rate charged on the
employee's corporate card statement. The method used must be consistent
and documented.

10.7 Employees must not submit duplicate claims, split transactions to
avoid thresholds, alter receipts, or claim personal expenses as business
expenses.

## 11. Corporate Cards and Cash Advances

11.1 Employees who travel at least **three (3) times per year** may be
eligible for a Corporate American Express / Visa card with manager
endorsement and Finance approval.

11.2 Corporate cards are for authorized business expenses only. Personal
charges are prohibited under all circumstances.

11.3 If an incidental personal charge occurs, the employee must identify
it and settle it within **seven (7) days**. The employee must not
disguise or net the charge against another business expense.

11.4 Lost, stolen, or compromised cards must be reported immediately to
the card issuer and Finance. Employees must review statements and report
unauthorized charges promptly.

11.5 Emergency cash advances are restricted to international travel in
cash-dominant economies, capped at **\$500 USD**, and require Finance
Director sign-off.

11.6 Advances must be reconciled with receipts and unused cash returned
within the expense-submission period or another written Finance
deadline.

## 12. Non-Reimbursable Expenses

Unless a written exception or applicable law requires otherwise, the
following are not reimbursable: - Personal purchases, personal
entertainment, or personal travel. - Commuting between home and the
permanent office. - Airline or hotel upgrades prohibited by this
policy. - Alcohol during individual meals under the standard per diem. -
Premium rideshare categories without approved justification. - Fines,
penalties, avoidable late fees, and expenses caused by misconduct. -
Expenses for spouses, family members, or other companions. - Duplicate,
unsupported, altered, or fraudulent claims. - Any expense prohibited by
law or the Code of Business Conduct.

## 13. Safety, Security, and Duty of Care

13.1 Travelers must follow travel advisories, company security guidance,
local laws, and reasonable safety instructions.

13.2 Employees must protect passports, devices, corporate cards,
confidential papers, and credentials. Lost devices or suspected data
exposure must be reported under the Information Security and BYOD
policies.

13.3 The traveler must notify the manager and designated travel support
promptly about illness, accident, detention, major disruption, or safety
threats.

## 14. Audit, Monitoring, and Fraud Prevention

14.1 Finance may review expense claims before or after payment, compare
booking data with claims, request supporting evidence, and recover
duplicate or ineligible payments where lawful.

14.2 High-risk indicators include repeated round-dollar claims, missing
receipts, duplicate invoices, split purchases, unusual vendor patterns,
personal weekend extensions, and claims submitted by the approver.

14.3 Approval and payment duties should be separated. No employee may
approve their own expense or alter supporting evidence.

14.4 Suggested metrics include on-time submission rate, percentage of
claims with complete receipts, average cost per trip, exception
frequency, duplicate claim rate, and corporate-card reconciliation age.

## 15. Exceptions, Violations, and Review

15.1 Exceptions must be written, justified, approved by the correct
authority before the expense, and retained with the expense report.
Emergency exceptions require prompt retrospective review.

15.2 Violations may result in rejection or recovery of expenses, card
suspension, retraining, disciplinary action up to termination, contract
remedies, or referral to authorities, subject to law and due process.

15.3 Finance reviews this policy annually and whenever travel markets,
tax rules, systems, or business requirements materially change.

## 16. Policy RAG and Interpretation Rules

-   Quote the exact limit and section; distinguish a daily combined cap
    from an individual meal cap.
-   Do not assume an expense is reimbursable merely because a manager
    approved it.
-   If destination tier, local tax treatment, or the current mileage
    rate is unclear, refer to Finance.
-   Do not invent receipt exceptions, approval authorities, or booking
    vendors.
-   Where the Code of Conduct is stricter for gifts or entertainment,
    apply the stricter requirement.
-   If a topic is not covered, say **"This policy does not specify that
    expense; Finance approval is required before commitment."**
