## 1. Purpose, Scope, and Principles

### 1.1 Purpose

This policy establishes consistent procedures for annual vacation,
public holidays, sick leave, parental leave, bereavement leave, civic
leave, unpaid personal leave, sabbaticals, absence reporting, and final
leave settlement.

### 1.2 Scope

It applies to employees of the organization across headquarters,
regional offices, and approved remote work locations. Contractors and
agency workers are covered only where their contract or applicable law
provides an entitlement.

### 1.3 Principles

The organization recognizes that rest, recovery, health, family
responsibilities, and civic obligations support employee well-being and
operational performance. Leave decisions must be timely, consistent,
non-discriminatory, confidential, and compliant with law.

### 1.4 Legal Priority

Where a law, employment agreement, collective agreement, or approved
local schedule provides greater leave or stronger protection, that
requirement takes precedence. HR must not use this policy to reduce a
statutory entitlement.

## 2. Definitions

-   **Calendar day:** A day counted on the calendar, including weekends
    and holidays.
-   **Working day:** A day the employee is scheduled to work.
-   **Annual leave:** Paid vacation leave accrued under this policy.
-   **Sick leave:** Approved absence for illness, injury, mental health,
    wellness, or permitted caregiving support.
-   **Accrual:** Leave earned over time.
-   **Carry-over:** Eligible unused leave moved into a subsequent leave
    year.
-   **Blackout period:** A limited period when operational coverage is
    constrained and leave may require additional coordination.
-   **Continuous service:** Uninterrupted employment recognized under
    the applicable contract and law.
-   **HRIS:** The approved human resources information system used to
    record balances, requests, approvals, and absences.

## 3. Roles and Responsibilities

### 3.1 Employee

Employees must maintain accurate leave requests, provide reasonable
notice, follow absence notification procedures, submit required
documentation, and check their available balance before making
non-refundable personal commitments.

### 3.2 Manager

Managers must assess coverage, apply rules consistently, respond within
the stated timeframe, document decisions, avoid arbitrary denial, and
escalate sensitive or legally protected requests to HR.

### 3.3 HR Operations

HR maintains balances, eligibility rules, records, local legal
schedules, and escalation procedures. HR advises managers on protected
leave, accommodations, documentation, and complex cases.

### 3.4 Payroll

Payroll applies approved paid/unpaid status, accrual adjustments,
statutory deductions, and final settlement in accordance with HR
instructions and applicable law.

### 3.5 Legal

Legal reviews statutory conflicts, protected leave, accommodation
issues, disputed deductions, and local-law requirements.

## 4. Annual Leave Entitlement and Accrual

### 4.1 Full-Time Permanent Employees

Full-time permanent employees accrue **twenty (20) working days of paid
annual leave per full calendar year** for service in the 0--2 year band.

### 4.2 Tenure-Based Allocation

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Continuous service         Annual entitlement       Approximate monthly
                                                                  accrual
  ------------------- ------------------------- -------------------------
  0 to 2 years              **20 working days**                 1.67 days

  3 to 4 years              **23 working days**                 1.92 days

  5 or more years           **25 working days**                 2.08 days
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

The source policy displays 1.66, 1.91, and 2.08 days per month. HRIS
must calculate accrual using a documented, consistent precision and
rounding method so that the annual total equals the approved
entitlement. For a standard 40-hour workweek, the source policy states
an equivalent of approximately **13.33 hours per month** for the 20-day
annual entitlement.

### 4.3 Accrual Timing

Leave accrues on the last day of each completed calendar month of active
service. HR must define how unpaid leave, partial months, joiners,
leavers, and statutory protected absences affect accrual in each local
schedule.

### 4.4 Part-Time Employees

Part-time employees receive pro-rated annual leave based on contracted
weekly hours compared with the standard **40-hour full-time schedule**.
HRIS must show the calculation basis and balance.

### 4.5 Fixed-Term Employees

Fixed-term employees receive annual leave in accordance with their
contract and applicable law. Where this policy applies, entitlement must
be pro-rated for the contract period. HR must confirm eligibility rather
than assume that all fixed-term arrangements are identical.

### 4.6 Probation

New hires begin accruing annual leave on **Day 1**. Accrued leave may be
requested during the **90-day probationary period**, subject to manager
approval and applicable law.

### 4.7 Balance Visibility

Employees should be able to view accrued, used, pending, carried-over,
and available leave in HRIS. HR must investigate material discrepancies
promptly.

## 5. Carry-Over, Expiration, and Encashment

5.1 Employees are encouraged to use annual leave during the year in
which it is earned.

5.2 A maximum of **five (5) unused annual leave days** may be carried
into the next calendar year.

5.3 Carried-over days must be used by **March 31** of the subsequent
year. Under the source policy, remaining carried-over days expire on
**April 1** without financial compensation. This expiration rule must
not be applied where prohibited by law or where the organization has
prevented the employee from taking leave.

5.4 Active employees may not cash out or sell back unused annual leave,
except where a local law or approved plan requires it.

5.5 On resignation or termination, accrued unused annual leave is
settled in accordance with Section 12 and applicable law.

5.6 HR must notify employees of balances approaching expiration and give
managers an opportunity to plan coverage so employees can use leave.

## 6. Request, Notice, Approval, and Scheduling

### 6.1 Required System

All annual leave requests must be submitted and tracked through **HRIS /
Workday**. Verbal or informal arrangements alone do not create an
approved absence. If the system is unavailable, HR may authorize a
temporary documented process and later enter the request.

### 6.2 Minimum Advance Notice

  Requested absence                            Minimum notice
  ------------------------------------ ----------------------
  1--2 consecutive working days           **2 business days**
  3--5 consecutive working days           **5 business days**
  6 or more consecutive working days     **10 business days**

These are planning targets and do not override emergency, disability,
protected, or statutory leave rights.

### 6.3 Manager Response

Managers must respond within **three (3) business days**. A response
must be approve, decline with a clear operational reason, or request
reasonable clarification. Silence is not approval.

### 6.4 Decision Criteria

Managers may consider: - Minimum safe staffing and customer-service
coverage. - Critical deadlines and operational cycles. - Previously
approved leave and fairness across the team. - Skills coverage and
handover arrangements. - Whether alternative dates would resolve a
genuine conflict.

Managers must not discriminate, retaliate, or deny leave arbitrarily.
Blackout windows must be limited, communicated in advance, and reviewed
by HR.

### 6.5 Changes and Cancellation

Employees should update HRIS promptly when dates change. Managers must
not cancel approved leave without a legitimate business reason and HR
review. Any unavoidable cancellation should consider the employee's
non-refundable costs and applicable law.

### 6.6 Public Holidays

An approved annual-leave day that falls on a recognized national or
company public holiday is not deducted from the employee's annual leave
balance.

## 7. Sick Leave and Medical Certification

### 7.1 Allocation

Full-time employees receive **ten (10) paid sick and wellness days per
calendar year**, available from the first day of employment. Part-time
and fixed-term eligibility must be determined by the applicable local
schedule, contract, and law.

### 7.2 Notification

For sudden illness, the employee must notify their direct manager
through Slack or email by **9:00 AM local time** on each morning of
absence, where practicable. If the employee cannot use those channels, a
phone call or another approved method may be used. Emergency
circumstances are not grounds for unreasonable delay or punishment.

### 7.3 Medical Certificate

A certificate from a qualified medical practitioner is required under
the source policy for: - Continuous sick absence exceeding **three (3)
consecutive working days**; or - A sick day immediately adjacent to
scheduled annual leave or a public holiday.

HR must apply this requirement consistently and only to the extent
permitted by local law. Medical documentation should normally confirm
the need and expected duration of absence, not disclose a diagnosis or
unnecessary clinical details.

### 7.4 Mental Health and Caregiver Support

Sick leave may be used for mental-health recovery, psychological
well-being, and personal caregiver support where permitted by applicable
law and the employee's eligibility.

### 7.5 Return to Work

Where an absence may affect safe performance, HR may request a lawful
fitness-for-work assessment or reasonable work restrictions. Managers
must not request unnecessary medical details or discuss an employee's
health with colleagues.

### 7.6 Extended or Repeated Absence

HR should review extended or repeated absence confidentially, consider
statutory leave, reasonable accommodation, occupational health, and
available support, and avoid assumptions about misconduct.

## 8. Parental Leave

### 8.1 Primary Caregiver Leave

Eligible full-time employees with at least **six (6) months of
continuous service** receive **sixteen (16) weeks of fully paid leave at
100% of base pay** following birth, adoption, or surrogacy, as described
in the source policy.

### 8.2 Secondary Caregiver Leave

Eligible secondary caregivers receive **six (6) weeks of fully paid
leave at 100% of base pay**. The leave may be taken continuously or in
two separate blocks within the child's first **12 months**.

### 8.3 Benefits Continuity

The source policy provides continued medical, dental, life-insurance,
and pension contributions throughout parental leave. HR and Benefits
must confirm how each plan and local law applies to employee
contributions and coverage.

### 8.4 Notice and Documentation

Employees should notify HR as early as reasonably possible so that
coverage and benefits can be planned. HR may request reasonable
supporting documentation permitted by law. Sensitive family and medical
information must be restricted.

### 8.5 Non-Retaliation

No employee may be penalized for requesting or taking legally protected
parental leave. Return-to-work rights and role protection are governed
by applicable law and the approved local schedule.

## 9. Bereavement, Civic, and Public-Service Leave

9.1 **Immediate family bereavement:** **Five (5) paid days** for the
death of a spouse, domestic partner, child, parent, sibling,
grandparent, or grandchild.

9.2 **Extended-relative bereavement:** Up to **two (2) paid days** for
the death of an aunt, uncle, in-law, or first cousin.

9.3 **Jury duty and court witness service:** Up to **ten (10) business
days of paid leave per calendar year** for compulsory jury summons or
court witness appearances, upon provision of the official court notice.
Local legal requirements govern where they provide a different
entitlement.

9.4 **Voting leave:** Up to **two (2) consecutive hours of paid time
off** on official national election voting days where the employee's
schedule prevents reasonable access to polling.

9.5 HR may request proportionate documentation, but should not collect
more personal or family information than necessary.

## 10. Unpaid Personal Leave and Sabbaticals

10.1 Employees may request up to **thirty (30) calendar days** of unpaid
personal leave for emergency personal reasons, subject to Department
Head approval and applicable law.

10.2 Employees with at least **three (3) consecutive years of service**
and in good standing may apply for an unpaid career sabbatical of **one
(1) to three (3) months** for academic research, community volunteerism,
or skill development.

10.3 The source policy states that job role and seniority level are
guaranteed upon return from an approved sabbatical. HR must document
this commitment in the written approval and confirm any local legal or
organizational constraints before approval.

10.4 The approval must state start/end dates, benefits treatment,
accrual treatment, contact expectations, return arrangements, and any
required handover.

## 11. Leave Administration, Records, and Privacy

11.1 HRIS is the authoritative record for balances, requests, approvals,
cancellations, and leave status.

11.2 Access to medical and family documentation is limited to
HR/authorized Benefits personnel and others with a legitimate legal or
operational need.

11.3 Managers should receive only the information necessary to schedule
work, such as approved dates and work restrictions---not detailed
diagnoses.

11.4 HR must retain leave records according to the approved
records-retention schedule and applicable employment law.

11.5 Payroll must receive timely, accurate updates for paid, unpaid, and
statutory absence treatment.

## 12. Resignation, Termination, and Final Settlement

12.1 Upon voluntary resignation or involuntary termination, accrued but
unused annual leave through the final service date is paid in the final
paycheck at the employee's **base daily rate**, subject to applicable
law and plan rules.

12.2 If an employee has used more annual leave than accrued on a
pro-rata basis at departure, the source policy permits recovery of the
monetary value of the deficit from final compensation. Any deduction
must first be reviewed by HR, Payroll, and Legal and may be made only
where lawful and authorized.

12.3 HR must provide Payroll with the final balance calculation, accrual
period, days taken, carry-over, and applicable daily-rate method.

12.4 No deduction or forfeiture may be made in violation of
wage-payment, leave, or final-pay laws.

## 13. Exceptions, Disputes, and Compliance

13.1 Exceptions require written HR approval and, where needed, Legal
review. A manager cannot waive statutory rights.

13.2 Employees may raise concerns about an incorrect balance,
inconsistent treatment, or denied request with HR. HR must review the
record and provide a reasoned response.

13.3 Deliberate falsification of leave records or misuse of leave may
result in disciplinary action after a fair review. Good-faith illness,
protected leave, disability, or a request for accommodation must not be
treated as misconduct.

## 14. Monitoring and Review

Suggested metrics: - Leave requests answered within three business
days. - Annual leave utilization and carry-over balances. -
Leave-balance correction rate. - Absence coverage and overtime
impacts. - Protected-leave cases handled within required timeframes. -
Employee complaints and resolution time.

HR reviews this policy annually and after material legal changes.

## 15. Policy RAG and Interpretation Rules

-   Always distinguish annual leave, sick leave, parental leave, and
    unpaid leave.
-   Quote the employee category and eligibility condition; do not assume
    part-time or fixed-term entitlements where the policy is silent.
-   The 20/23/25-day schedule applies to the stated tenure bands; use
    HRIS for the exact balance.
-   Do not treat a manager's silence as approval.
-   Do not disclose or infer medical diagnoses.
-   Statutory rights and local schedules override this global baseline
    where more favorable.
-   If the answer is not specified, say **"This policy does not specify
    the entitlement; HR must confirm the employee's local schedule and
    contract."**
