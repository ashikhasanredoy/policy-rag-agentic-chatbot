## 1. Purpose and Total Rewards Principles

1.1 The organization's Total Rewards program is intended to support
employees' physical, mental, financial, and professional well-being
through compensation, insurance, retirement, education, and wellness
support.

1.2 Benefits are administered consistently, transparently, and in
accordance with plan documents and applicable law.

1.3 No manager may promise a benefit, coverage level, tax treatment, or
payment that is not authorized by the plan or this policy.

1.4 If this policy conflicts with a formal insurance contract,
retirement plan, statutory requirement, or employment agreement,
HR/Legal must determine the controlling provision. Employees must be
informed of material differences.

## 2. Definitions

-   **Eligible employee:** An employee meeting the employment category,
    service, location, and plan criteria.
-   **Eligible dependent:** A spouse, domestic partner, or dependent
    child who meets the applicable plan definition and age/eligibility
    rules.
-   **Plan year:** The period defined by the applicable benefit plan.
-   **Premium:** The amount charged for insurance coverage.
-   **Deductible:** The amount an insured person pays before plan
    coverage begins, as defined by the plan.
-   **Copayment / coinsurance:** The member's fixed or percentage share
    of a covered service.
-   **HSA:** Health Savings Account, available only where the employee
    and plan meet applicable US legal requirements.
-   **401(k):** A US employer-sponsored retirement savings arrangement
    subject to plan terms and applicable law.
-   **EAP:** Employee Assistance Program providing confidential support
    services.
-   **Base salary:** The employee's regular base compensation, excluding
    variable or discretionary payments unless a plan expressly includes
    them.

## 3. Eligibility, Enrollment, and Administration

### 3.1 Source Eligibility Date

The source policy states that full-time regular employees and eligible
dependents become eligible for health, retirement, and wellness benefits
on the **first day of the calendar month following the date of hire**.

### 3.2 Part-Time Eligibility

The policy applies to regular full-time and part-time employees, but the
source eligibility clause expressly describes full-time regular
employees. HR must publish a benefit-by-benefit eligibility schedule for
part-time employees based on plan documents and applicable law. Do not
infer that every benefit is available to every part-time employee.

### 3.3 Dependents

The source policy identifies eligible dependents as a spouse, domestic
partner, and dependent children up to age **26**. The plan administrator
must confirm the applicable definition, evidence requirements, and any
local-law variations.

### 3.4 Enrollment

HR/Benefits must provide employees with enrollment instructions,
coverage summaries, premium contributions, deadlines, and contact
details. Employees must submit complete and accurate enrollment
information.

### 3.5 Qualifying Life Events

Changes such as marriage, divorce, birth, adoption, loss of other
coverage, or dependent eligibility changes may create a special
enrollment opportunity where permitted by the plan and law. Employees
must notify Benefits within the applicable plan deadline and provide
required evidence.

### 3.6 Changes and Termination

Coverage changes, continuation rights, dependent removal, and coverage
end dates are governed by plan terms and applicable law. HR must
communicate the relevant dates and available continuation or conversion
options where required.

## 4. Medical, Dental, and Vision Coverage

### 4.1 Medical Plan Options

The source policy provides three medical-plan options:

**A. PPO Premium Plan** - Low deductible. - Comprehensive national
provider network. - Company pays **85% of employee premiums** and **70%
of dependent premiums**. - Employees are responsible for the remaining
premium share and applicable plan cost-sharing.

**B. High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP) with HSA** - Paired with a
Health Savings Account where legally eligible. - Company annual HSA
contribution: **\$1,000 USD for individual coverage** or **\$2,000 USD
for family coverage**. - HSA contributions and withdrawals must comply
with applicable tax rules and plan eligibility requirements.

**C. HMO Plan** - Integrated local provider network. - Zero-copay
preventive care as stated in the source policy, subject to the plan's
definition of preventive services and network rules.

### 4.2 Medical Plan Administration

The plan documents control covered services, network access, referrals,
deductibles, out-of-pocket limits, exclusions, prior authorization,
claims, appeals, and effective dates. Marketing summaries must not be
treated as a substitute for formal plan terms.

### 4.3 Dental Coverage

The source policy provides: - **100% coverage** for preventive cleanings
twice per year. - **80% coverage** for basic restorative procedures. -
**50% coverage** for major dental and orthodontic services. - Annual
maximum of **\$2,500 USD per covered individual**.

Coverage percentages are subject to network rules, eligible procedures,
waiting periods, annual maximums, and plan exclusions.

### 4.4 Vision Coverage

The source policy provides: - One annual comprehensive eye examination
with a **\$10 USD copay**. - Annual frame and contact-lens hardware
allowance of **\$250 USD** per calendar year.

The plan administrator must define eligible retailers, claim submission,
replacement frequency, and unused-allowance treatment.

### 4.5 Claims and Appeals

Employees should submit claims and supporting documents through the
insurer or plan administrator. Denied claims must include the reason and
appeal route. HR may assist with process questions but cannot guarantee
a claim outcome.

## 5. Retirement Savings and Employer Match

### 5.1 401(k) Match

The source policy provides a **50% company match on employee
contributions up to 6% of eligible base salary**, resulting in a maximum
employer contribution of **3% of eligible base salary**.

### 5.2 Automatic Enrollment

Eligible new employees are automatically enrolled at a **4% default
contribution rate 30 days after onboarding**, with the option to opt out
or change the contribution rate at any time, subject to plan procedures
and legal requirements.

### 5.3 Vesting

The source policy states that safe-harbor matching contributions are
**100% vested from Day 1**. The formal plan document must confirm the
contribution type and vesting treatment.

### 5.4 Employee Responsibilities

Employees should review contribution elections, investment choices,
beneficiary designations, fees, and tax implications. Employees must
keep beneficiary information current.

### 5.5 Administration and Compliance

Payroll must transmit eligible deductions and employer contributions
accurately and on time. Plan administrators must maintain required
notices, participant disclosures, testing, filings, and records.

## 6. Professional Development and Tuition Reimbursement

### 6.1 Eligibility and Annual Limit

Full-time employees with at least **twelve (12) months of continuous
service** may apply for reimbursement of up to **\$5,250 USD per
calendar year** for accredited undergraduate, graduate, or certified
professional credential programs relevant to their career trajectory.

### 6.2 Prior Approval

Employees must obtain written manager and HR/Benefits approval before
enrolling or committing non-refundable tuition, unless an approved
program states otherwise. Approval should confirm course relevance,
institution accreditation, budget, eligible expenses, and the maximum
reimbursable amount.

### 6.3 Grade Requirement

Reimbursement requires a minimum grade of **B (3.0 GPA)** or **Pass** in
an accredited pass/fail course.

### 6.4 Required Evidence

Employees must submit: - Prior approval. - Proof of enrollment and
course completion. - Itemized tuition invoice and proof of payment. -
Official grade report or pass confirmation. - Any tax documentation
required by Payroll.

Books, travel, equipment, late fees, and other expenses are excluded
unless the written approval expressly includes them.

### 6.5 Retention Commitment

The source policy requires employees receiving tuition reimbursement to
agree to remain with the company for at least **twelve (12) months
following course completion**. If the employee leaves earlier, the
source policy provides for pro-rated repayment. Any repayment agreement
must be separately documented, proportionate, and reviewed by Legal for
enforceability under applicable wage and employment laws. No unlawful
payroll deduction may be made.

### 6.6 Tax Treatment

The source policy references US Internal Revenue Code Section 127 and
the **\$5,250 USD** annual amount as tax-free. Payroll/Tax must verify
the current legal limit, program qualification, and treatment before
representing reimbursement as tax-free.

## 7. Life, Accidental Death, and Disability Insurance

### 7.1 Basic Life and AD&D

The source policy provides company-funded group term life and accidental
death and dismemberment coverage equal to **two (2) times base annual
salary**, up to a maximum benefit of **\$750,000 USD**.

### 7.2 Short-Term Disability

Following a **seven (7) day elimination period**, the source policy
provides short-term disability income replacement of **66.6% of base
pre-disability weekly salary for up to 26 weeks**, for an approved
medical disability.

### 7.3 Long-Term Disability

For continuous disability exceeding **180 days**, the source policy
provides long-term disability income replacement of **60% of base
monthly salary**, up to **\$10,000 USD per month**, until retirement
age, subject to plan terms.

### 7.4 Claims and Medical Information

Claims are assessed by the insurer or plan administrator. Medical
records must be handled confidentially and shared only with authorized
parties. HR should receive only information necessary to administer
leave, benefits, and work accommodations.

### 7.5 Coordination

STD, LTD, workers' compensation, statutory benefits, and other income
sources may interact. The applicable plan document determines offsets,
exclusions, and coordination rules.

## 8. Wellness and Employee Assistance Program

### 8.1 Monthly Wellness Stipend

Eligible employees may receive a taxable reimbursement of **\$75 USD per
month** for approved wellness expenses, including: - Gym memberships. -
Fitness-class subscriptions. - Yoga studios. - Meditation app
subscriptions such as Headspace or Calm. - Home fitness equipment.

### 8.2 Claim Requirements

Claims must be submitted through the designated expense or benefits
channel with proof of purchase. The company may define eligible items,
claim deadlines, and whether unused monthly amounts carry forward.
Unless a written plan says otherwise, unused amounts do not carry
forward.

### 8.3 Tax Treatment

The source policy describes this stipend as taxable. Payroll must apply
the correct tax and withholding treatment in the employee's
jurisdiction.

### 8.4 Employee Assistance Program

The EAP provides confidential counseling services with up to **eight (8)
free mental-health therapy sessions per issue per year** for employees
and their household members, available **24/7/365** as stated in the
source policy.

### 8.5 Confidentiality and Emergencies

EAP providers should not disclose counseling content to the employer
except as permitted by law, necessary for immediate safety, or
authorized by the individual. EAP is not a substitute for emergency
medical services. Employees facing immediate danger should contact local
emergency services.

## 9. Compensation and Payroll Governance

9.1 Individual base pay, variable pay, commissions, bonuses, and
allowances must be documented in the employee's offer letter,
compensation statement, or approved plan. This policy does not create a
salary amount or guarantee discretionary compensation.

9.2 Compensation changes require authorized approval, an effective date,
and payroll documentation.

9.3 Payroll must maintain accurate records of salary, deductions,
benefit contributions, tax withholding, and reimbursements in accordance
with local law.

9.4 Employees should review pay statements and report suspected errors
promptly. Payroll must investigate and correct confirmed errors in the
next practicable payroll cycle or sooner where law requires.

9.5 Compensation information is confidential business information, but
nothing in this policy restricts rights protected by applicable
pay-transparency or labor laws.

## 10. Equity, Accessibility, and Non-Discrimination

10.1 Eligibility and administration must be applied consistently and
without unlawful discrimination.

10.2 HR must consider reasonable accommodation and accessibility needs
in benefits communications, enrollment, wellness programs, and claims
processes.

10.3 Employees must not be disadvantaged for making a good-faith benefit
claim, requesting information, or raising a concern.

## 11. Records, Privacy, and Vendor Oversight

11.1 Benefits records may include enrollment forms, dependent
verification, claims status, contribution elections, reimbursements, and
tax records.

11.2 Access is limited to authorized HR, Payroll, Finance, plan
administrators, and insurers with a legitimate need.

11.3 Medical and disability information is treated as highly
confidential and separated from ordinary personnel records where
required.

11.4 Benefits vendors must undergo appropriate due diligence and
contract review. Contracts must address confidentiality, security, data
use, incident reporting, and data return/deletion.

## 12. Employee Questions, Appeals, and Exceptions

12.1 Employees should first contact HR/Benefits or the plan
administrator using the official contact information supplied during
enrollment.

12.2 Coverage or claim appeals must follow the insurer/plan's formal
process and deadlines.

12.3 Exceptions require written approval from HR/Benefits and Finance,
with Legal review where tax, plan, or employment rights are affected.

12.4 Managers may not promise a claim payment, change insurer terms, or
waive plan eligibility rules.

## 13. Monitoring and Review

Suggested metrics: - Enrollment completion and error rates. - Claims
turnaround and appeal outcomes where available. - Employer match and
contribution reconciliation. - Tuition reimbursement utilization and
documentation completeness. - Wellness reimbursement processing time. -
EAP access information availability, without collecting confidential
counseling content.

HR/Benefits reviews the policy annually and after material plan or legal
changes.

## 14. Policy RAG and Interpretation Rules

-   Distinguish policy design values from insurer or plan-document
    coverage.
-   Quote the exact percentage, dollar limit, eligibility group, and
    section.
-   Do not assume part-time eligibility where the plan documents are not
    supplied.
-   Do not promise tax-free treatment without Payroll/Tax confirmation.
-   Do not infer diagnoses or request counseling details.
-   If a plan term is not in this policy, say **"The policy does not
    specify this plan detail; consult the applicable plan document or
    HR/Benefits."**
