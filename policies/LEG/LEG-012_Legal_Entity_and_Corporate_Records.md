## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes the minimum governance, responsibilities, and control requirements for legal entity and corporate records across the
organization. It is intended to promote consistent decisions, reduce avoidable risk, protect stakeholders and organizational resources, and
create an auditable record of important activities.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to employees, officers, managers, temporary personnel, and other individuals acting on behalf of the organization in
connection with legal entity and corporate records. Relevant contractors, suppliers, and service providers are included where their activities,
access, or contracts bring them within scope. Applicable law and binding contractual obligations take precedence where they impose stricter
requirements.

## 3. Policy Statement and Principles

The organization requires legal entity and corporate records activities to be carried out in a controlled, transparent, risk-aware, and
consistently documented manner. Each material activity must have an accountable owner, appropriate authorization, reliable evidence, and a
route for escalation.
- Accountability: roles, decision rights, and control ownership are explicit.
- Proportionality: safeguards reflect the value, sensitivity, impact, and likelihood of risk.
- Traceability: approvals, decisions, exceptions, and material actions are recorded.
- Integrity: requirements are applied consistently and conflicts are disclosed.

## 4. Mandatory Requirements

- Legal and regulatory obligations must be identified, assigned to accountable owners, and reviewed when circumstances change.
- Material commitments, disclosures, investigations, and external responses require review and approval by authorized personnel.
- Conflicts, suspected misconduct, legal holds, and regulatory concerns must be escalated promptly and handled confidentially.
- Records must be accurate, preserved when required, and protected against unauthorized disclosure, alteration, or destruction.
- The accountable owner must document the purpose, scope, key risks, and control expectations for legal entity and corporate records.
- Records must be accurate, complete, access-controlled, and retained under the approved records schedule.
- Exceptions must be justified in writing, time-limited, risk-assessed, approved, and recorded in the exception register.

## 5. Operating Procedure

1. The business owner refers the matter to Legal or Compliance with relevant facts, dates, documents, and requested outcome.
2. Legal or Compliance assesses obligations, conflicts, deadlines, preservation needs, and potential exposure.
3. Authorized personnel approve advice, response, commitment, investigation step, or exception before action.
4. The owner records the outcome, preserves required evidence, and tracks continuing obligations.

## 6. Roles and Responsibilities

- Legal and Compliance Department: owns this policy, maintains procedures and evidence, monitors implementation, and proposes
updates.
- General Counsel / Chief Compliance Officer: approves this policy and material amendments within the governance framework.
- Managers and process owners: implement controls, assign competent personnel, review exceptions, and escalate material issues.
- Employees and relevant third parties: understand applicable requirements, complete assigned training, protect records, and report
concerns.
- Risk, Legal, Compliance, Internal Audit, and other control functions: provide advice, oversight, or independent assurance within their
mandates.

## 7. Records and Evidence

- Approvals, decisions, reviews, declarations, and exception records associated with this policy.
- Current procedures, checklists, training acknowledgements, role assignments, and relevant communications.
- Transaction, operational, incident, monitoring, reconciliation, and remediation evidence, as applicable.
- Records are stored in approved repositories and protected against unauthorized access, alteration, or destruction.

## 8. Monitoring and Assurance

The policy owner defines indicators and review frequency proportionate to risk. Monitoring may include completion and timeliness, error or
defect rates, exception volumes, incident trends, overdue actions, access or approval reviews, service outcomes, and stakeholder feedback.
Material failures require an accountable owner, target date, and documented remediation plan. Independent assurance may test control
design and operating effectiveness.

## 9. Exceptions

An exception requires a written rationale, defined scope and duration, risk assessment, compensating controls, accountable owner, and
approval by the designated authority. It must be entered in the policy exception register and reviewed before expiry. No exception may
authorize unlawful conduct or override a mandatory safety, regulatory, or contractual obligation.

## 10. Non-Compliance and Escalation

Suspected breaches must be reported to the manager, policy owner, or designated reporting channel. The organization will assess concerns
fairly, preserve relevant evidence, and apply proportionate corrective action consistent with applicable law and related procedures. Retaliation
against good-faith reporting is prohibited. Immediate safety, security, legal, or financial risks must be escalated without delay.
